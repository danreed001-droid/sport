- **Missouri +5.5 at Ole Miss, L 3-31 (-22.5, clean).** The main support was SP+ calling
  it nearly a pick'em against a 5.5 market. That's a public rating the market had already

Indiana +3.5 lost by half a point (19-15), and it was the one clean pick built on reverse line

## Is "shade halfway to the line" helping?

- **It changes how many picks get made, not which side.** Halving the gap to the line keeps
  the projection on the same side, so the forced-side record is the same as an unshaded
  projection would have had. Projection error was 9.80 points per game against 9.82 for
  the line (9.88 unshaded): the projection is still no sharper than the line.
- **As a filter it saved money.** Picks fell from 22 of 34 games to 6 of 30. The 17 passes
  with a forced side went 7-9-1 (clean 3-4-1). The 10 passes whose unshaded edge was 2-4
  points, the picks the old rule would have made, went 4-6. Under the Week 5 rule those would
  have been picks at about a coin flip, paying the vig.
- **Bigger unshaded edges did better.** Over Weeks 5-6, games with an unshaded edge of 4+
  points went 5-1 on the forced side (clean 4-1: Notre Dame, USC, Texas Tech and Kentucky won;
  Missouri lost). Edges under 4 went 20-27-1 (clean 10-16-2). Six games is far too few to
  trust, but it points the same way as the shading rule. Verdict: keep shading, and treat it
  as a pick threshold (an unshaded edge of 4+, or 2+ after shading), not as a correction that
  sharpens the number.
- The rule was bent once. Cleveland was taken at a shaded edge of 1.5, under the 2-point
  band, and that lean was formed after a search summary implied Baltimore won.

## Patterns

- **A favorite as the projection's side was the weak spot.** Over Weeks 5-6 the forced side
  went 7-13-2 when it was the favorite (clean 4-10-2) and 18-15 when it was the underdog.
  This week favorites finished 1.2 points short of their projection on average (CFB 1.9).
- **"The underdog can't score" didn't hold.** Reasoning that leaned on a weak underdog
  offense went 2-5-1:
  - covered: Texas Tech over Arizona State, Kansas City over the Chargers (contaminated);
  - failed: Auburn, BYU, Tennessee (CFB) and the Tennessee Titans all won outright, and
    Colorado covered (contaminated);
  - push: the Jets.

  A weak offense is already in the spread. The Week 5 lesson about not fading a favorite
  because the opponent's offense is weak appears to have been stretched into "back the
  favorite when the dog's offense is weak".
- **Favorites whose case was their defense flopped.** Texas (2nd in adjusted EPA on defense,
  55th in offensive success rate) lost outright at -11.5. Oklahoma (the top adjusted
  defense, 127th in offensive success rate) lost outright at -6.5. Georgia (no opponent
  over 20, but scored 20 at Alabama) lost outright at -19.5. All three were 0-3 ATS.
- **Big line moves: the moved side kept covering.** In games with a 3+ point move, the side
  the line moved toward went 9-1-1 (clean 5-1-1):
  - covered: SMU, Michigan State, LSU, Texas Tech, Colorado, Carolina, Chicago, Green Bay,
    New Orleans;
  - push: New England;
  - failed: Indianapolis.

  Week 5's picks with the move went 1-4. Together the record says there's no reliable
  direction. The safe part of the lesson is not to fade a move and not to count its cause
  twice.
- **A stale slate line was the cleanest edge.** Texas Tech's slate number was -14.5 while
  the market closed -16.5, and the pick won by 13.5 (clean). Cleveland +4.5 (market 3.5)
  and Las Vegas +5.5 (market 4.5) also won, but both are contaminated. All three got a
  number better than the current market.
- **Public ratings that disagree with the line by 2.5+ went 3-6 (clean 2-3).** Covered:
  Tennessee, Texas Tech, Wake Forest. Failed: Missouri, Northwestern, Texas, Utah, Virginia
  and the Rams' pundit projections. Ratings everyone has don't beat the line.
- **Reverse line movement toward the underdog:** Indiana lost by 0.5 (clean), Washington's
  pass would have covered (clean), and Las Vegas won (contaminated). Over Weeks 5-6 it's
  5-2 (clean 4-1). Toward the favorite: Georgia and Houston (passes) failed and New Orleans
  covered (contaminated), so 1-4 over the two weeks.
- **[angle] calls:**

  - Hurt: Simmons's return to Oxford (Missouri), and Ohio State's reshuffled line against
