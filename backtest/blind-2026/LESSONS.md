# Lessons for the blind test (cumulative; updated after Week 9)

Read these before researching. Each lesson ends with its evidence. "Clean" means the result
wasn't seen before locking.

**The bottom line after five weeks:**
- The clean forced side is 115-130-3 (46.9%), and picks are 16-18-1 (-$348).
- No split by sport, favorite/underdog, home/road, line size, line move, slate-vs-close or
  projected gap is reliably different from a coin flip once you allow for the ~40 cells checked.
- Passing is the normal outcome. A pick needs a concrete reason the market plausibly hasn't
  priced, checked against the close. A rule, a market signal or the size of the number is not
  that reason.

## Process

- **Keep results out of your searches.**
  - The slate already has the lines, so don't search for odds or line movement.
  - Don't open or rely on summaries of these, because they list finals or graded picks:
    - Dimers game pages;
    - Covers matchup, team, schedule and results pages, and Covers pick roundups;
    - SBR odds pages;
    - SportsGambler head-to-head lists;
    - Pickswise matchup pages.
  - Limiting a search to a betting site doesn't protect you, because those are the sites that
    leak.
  - Once a lean is written down, stop searching that game.
  - Evidence: contaminated games were 13 of 34 (Wk5), 12 of 30 (Wk6), 27 of 83 (Wk7), 26 of 94
    (Wk8) and 23 of 121 (Wk9).
- **Later previews leak this game, and Predictem is now the worst source.** Searches that match a
  team's next game or a rematch return this result. Put the game's date and "preview" in every
  query, and only use articles dated that day or earlier. Be most careful with NCAAB in
  November: teams play two or three times a week, so the next preview comes out within days.
  Evidence:
  - about 13 of 27 leaks in Wk7 and 14 of 26 in Wk8 came from later previews;
  - in Wk9, about 11 of 23 came through predictem.com-limited searches;
  - NCAAB had 12 of 43 games contaminated in Wk9.
- **If you see any hint of the result before your lean is written down, lock a pass with "no
  read".** Never fill the forced side after a leak. Evidence: in Wk9 all 23 were locked that
  way. In Wk7-8 about 19 sides were filled after the leak by mechanical rules.
- **Honor your own pass trigger.** If your reasoning names a status that would turn the pick into
  a pass, confirm it from the final availability report or pass. Evidence: the Wk5 Saints lost
  after Jefferson played. The Wk6-7 passes on unresolved status were right.
- **Check the archive's lines.**
  - Don't use `homeSpreadOpen`. It's sometimes sign-flipped or a placeholder. Evidence: 7 of 94
    (Wk8) and 9 of 121 (Wk9) were sign-opposite to the close or 5+ away, for example Houston @
    Chargers -1.5 → +5.5.
  - `homeSpreadClose` is usually right but not always:
    - Wk9 Denver @ Houston: archive -4.5, reported -8.5 on the day;
    - Wk9 Dallas: archive -7.5, books -8.5 to -9.5.

    If the slate and the close differ by 3+, treat it as a data flag and don't build a case on
    either number. Evidence: in those 12 Wk9 games the slate was nearer the result in 8.

## Weighing evidence (all sports)

- **Judge any edge against the close, not the slate.** News that moved the close is priced, even
  if it came after the slate's line. Evidence: both picks built on a slate number the close had
  moved past lost (Wk8 Atlanta, Wk9 Milwaukee on game-day injuries, which lost by 14). Clean
  forced sides with a 1+ better slate number went 26-37 in Wk8-9.
- **Your projection isn't sharper than the line, and a bigger gap doesn't mean a better side.**
  Keep the bar (a pick needs an unshaded gap of 4+, or 2+ after shading halfway to the line) as a
  filter only. Evidence:
  - the projection's average miss was 10.49 vs the line's 9.65 in Wk9 (11.03 vs 10.86 in Wk8);
  - clean forced sides with a gap of 2+ went 26-34-3 over Wk5-9 (5-14-2 in Wk9).
- **Don't default to the underdog.** Your projections lean toward the dog every week, and it
  hasn't paid. The gap isn't significant, so don't flip to favorites either: with no read, say
  "no read". In a near pick'em, a forced underdog is the weakest cell seen, but that was found by
  looking. Evidence:
  - 59% of clean forced sides were underdogs, and the average projection leaned to the dog in
    all five weeks;
  - forced underdogs went 62-82 (43.1%) and forced favorites 53-48;
  - underdogs on lines of 0-4.5 went 19-36.
- **Big college underdogs (25+) are not a bias to correct, and not an edge either.** Pace, a
  short bench or "the favorite will clear its bench" are priced. Evidence: NCAAB forced underdogs
  of 25+ went 5-7-1, about the rate of all such underdogs (13-16). NCAAB's 10-19-1 overall is
  scattered across line sizes (p=0.14).
- **Line moves and reverse line movement point nowhere.** Treat the close as fair. Don't fade it,
  and don't re-list the news that caused it as a reason. Evidence:
  - the side a 3+ point move went to covered 15-9-1 in Wk6-7, then 7-10 in Wk8;
  - reverse movement toward a favorite went 1-7 clean over Wk5-7.
- **No home/road or favorite/underdog tiebreaker in any sport.** These base-rate runs flip from
  week to week:
  - CFB home teams covered 27 of 35 in Wk7-8, then 4 of 16 in Wk9;
  - favorites finished about 3 points worse than the line in Wk5-6, then 3 points better in Wk9;
  - NFL road teams covered 44-26 over Wk5-9, but 4 of 14 in Wk8, and that's one cell of about
    20.
- **Injuries reported before the line settled are priced. Only news the close hasn't absorbed
  counts.** Evidence:
  - picks on the opponent's known absences went 0-3 (Wk7 Sacramento, Wk5 Texas A&M and Las
    Vegas);
  - the Wk9 Milwaukee pick on news the close had already moved 4 points on lost;
  - Wk6 Kentucky, on game-day news, won.
- **Already priced, so never a reason (tiebreaker at most):**
  - public ratings, models and preview writers' picks (Wk6 ratings 2-3, Wk7 Kings picks lost by
    20.5);
  - team ATS records;
  - head-to-head and venue history;
  - a low total or slow pace "making the points worth more" (3-7 in Wk5-7);
  - "the underdog can't score" (2-5-1 in Wk6);
  - one-unit preview labels.

## CFB

- **Don't lay points with a favorite whose case is its defense while its offense is middling or
  worse, but don't fade one on that alone.** Evidence: such favorites missed in 4 of 6 (Wk6
  Texas, Oklahoma and Georgia, and Wk8 Ohio State). Wk7 UCLA and Wk8 Texas covered. Fading on it
  alone went 1-2.

## NBA and NCAAB (early season)

- **Early in the season, expect no edge and pass.** Evidence:
  - the NBA clean forced side went 15-15, 17-20 and 19-22 in Wk7-9;
  - NCAAB went 10-19-1;
  - season openers, exhibitions, rebuilt rosters and one-game line moves were coin flips or
    worse (rebuilt-roster or returning-player bullets 5-13-1 in Wk9).
